# Tea today, in my communities
<img src="/images/tea/tea.png" alt="Tea gathering" style="float:left; width:31%; max-width:360px; margin:0.5rem 1rem 0.5rem 0;">

For many years I have been drawn to practices that invite people to slow down, pay attention and experience the world more fully. Tea has steadily become one of the most meaningful of these practices.


My tea gatherings are inspired by Chá Dào, the Way of Tea, a contemplative art form rooted in Chinese tea traditions. At its heart, tea is not simply something to drink; it is a way of gathering and sharing together. It is also about fine tuning our senses, paying attention and creating space for genuine human connection.

For as long as I can recall I have been fascinated by the role of ritual in our lives. Across cultures and throughout history, people have come together around shared rituals that help mark transitions, strengthen community and bring meaning to everyday experience. In a fast-moving world, these opportunities to pause and gather feel increasingly valuable.

A tea gathering offers a simple but powerful form of ritual. People come together, often as strangers, and through the shared experience of preparing, pouring and drinking tea, something new emerges. Conversations unfold. Silence becomes comfortable. Attention deepens. A temporary community is created that did not exist before the gathering began. A space opens up.

The teas I share are predominantly high-quality Chinese and Taiwanese teas, chosen for their character, seasonality and ability to invite a richer sensory experience. During gatherings I often share stories about where the tea was grown, the landscapes from which it comes, the people who cultivate it and the traditions that surround it. These stories become another way of exploring our relationship with place, history, plant and culture.

![tea](/images/tea/IMG_4743.jpeg)
<div style="text-align: right; font-size: small">Tea with the trees, April 2026</div>

Just as every landscape has its own character, every tea carries traces of the environment from which it emerged. Climate, altitude, soil, weather and human care all leave their mark. Drinking tea can therefore become an act of attention not only to flavour, but also to the landscapes, communities and histories that have shaped it.

My approach is gentle, welcoming and non-performative. No prior knowledge of tea is required. The emphasis is not on expertise but on intention: slowing down, engaging the senses and sharing a moment of beauty and creativity with others.

These gatherings bring together many strands of my wider practice, including creativity, mindfulness, nature connection and community. Whether held indoors, outdoors, beside water, beneath trees or around a table, each gathering is an invitation to pause, connect and discover what becomes possible when we slow down and give our full attention to the present moment.

I now offer public tea gatherings, private group experiences and creative tea-based events that bring together contemplation, community and connection to place.

Information about upcoming gatherings and future tea projects will be shared here soon.
